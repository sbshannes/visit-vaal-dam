Welcome to the Vaal

A playground of water, history and nature

Long before it was a weekend escape, this was a river valley of farms, drifts and stone kraals. In the 1930s, in the middle of the Great Depression, a wall went up across the Vaal River, and the valley slowly filled into one of the biggest bodies of water in the country.

Today the Vaal Dam is where Gauteng comes to breathe. Yachts heel over in the afternoon breeze, anglers sit out the night beside their rods, and fish eagles call across the bays. It is also a place of remarkable stories: flying boats that once landed here from England, an island owned by a university, and old farm graves that rise from the water when the level drops.

Plan your visit

Closer than you think

From Johannesburg it is roughly 100 kilometres, or about an hour and a half on the road, to Vaal Marina or Deneysville. Pretoria and OR Tambo are around two hours away. Come for a day, a long weekend or the whole school holiday.

The dam is never the same twice. In wet years the sluices roar; in dry years the water pulls back and reveals old foundations, farm walls and graves. Check the weekly dam level before you go.

Travel times, seasons & safety

The water is waiting. Come and find your Vaal.

Pack the cooler box, load the rods or the wakeboard, and point the car south. Whether it is a sunrise at Vaal Marina, a sail out of Deneysville, a quiet weekend at Oranjeville or a stroll past Frankfort's sandstone monuments, the Vaal Dam is closer than you think.
