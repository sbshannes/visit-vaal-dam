Plan your visit to the Vaal Dam

Travel times, the best seasons, water levels and how to stay safe on South Africa’s inland sea.

Getting here

The Vaal Dam lies on the border of Gauteng, the Free State and Mpumalanga. The dam is about 77 kilometres south of OR Tambo International Airport as the crow flies, and Vaal Marina and Deneysville are each roughly 100 kilometres by road from central Johannesburg. There is no public transport to speak of, so most visitors drive.

When to visit

Summer (October to March) is peak season: hot days, warm water and the sailing calendar in full swing, including the Round the Island Race on the first weekend of February. Highveld afternoons often bring dramatic thunderstorms, so plan water time for the mornings.

Autumn (April and May) brings settled, golden weather, quieter weekends and superb light for photographers.

Winter (June to August) is dry, sunny and crisp, with cold, sometimes frosty mornings. Fewer people, clear skies and good birding make it a lovely time for a cosy weekend away.

Spring (September) warms things up again before the rains return. Late in a dry winter is also when the dam is most likely to be low.

Water levels: the dam that changes its face

The Vaal Dam can be overflowing one year and well below half full a few years later. When it is very full, the open sluice gates are a spectacle worth the drive. When it is low, slipways may be left high and dry, but the receding water reveals history: old farm walls, the foundations of the flying-boat terminal and the graves on UJ Island.

Check the current level before you go:

  • Department of Water and Sanitation: weekly state of dams
  • DWS: Free State dams this week (includes the Vaal Dam)

Safety on the water

  • Wear a life jacket on any boat, ski or paddle craft, children especially.
  • Watch the weather. Summer storms build fast, bring lightning and strong gusts, and can whip up surprisingly big waves on a dam this size. Head for shore when the sky darkens.
  • Make sure your skipper and craft are legal. Powered craft need the right registration and skipper qualifications; check the current requirements before launching.
  • Know the water. Levels change, and submerged trees, rocks and old structures can lurk just below the surface, especially when the dam is low.
  • No alcohol at the helm, and keep a sober eye on swimmers.
  • Tell someone your plans before heading out, and carry a charged phone in a waterproof pouch.

Etiquette

  • Give sailing boats and anglers plenty of room, and slow down near moorings, swimmers and the shore.
  • Respect private property, yacht-club and resort rules, and pay launch fees where they apply.
  • Take your litter home; fishing line and plastic kill birds.
  • Heritage sites and graves exposed at low water are protected and many are on private land. Look, photograph, never remove.
  • Keep the music at a level your neighbours, and the fish eagles, can enjoy.

Emergency numbers

  • 112: national emergency number from any mobile phone
  • 087 094 9774: NSRI Emergency Operations Centre (sea and inland water rescue, 24 hours). The NSRI’s Station 22 is based at Deneysville.
  • 10111: SAPS emergency
