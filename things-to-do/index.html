Things to do at the Vaal Dam

Things to do on the Vaal Dam and the Vaal River

Sailing, fishing, skiing, birding, hiking and slow riverside days, plus the best of the river above and below the dam.

On the water

Boating, sailing & yacht racing

The Vaal Dam is one of the great inland sailing waters of South Africa. With hundreds of square kilometres of open water and reliable afternoon breezes, it is home to several yacht clubs, most of them around Deneysville and Vaal Marina, and on summer weekends the bays fill with sails.

The highlight of the calendar is the Round the Island Race, hosted by Lake Deneys Yacht Club over the first weekend of February since the late 1950s. In 2007 it set the Guinness World Record for the most yachts to finish an inland yacht race: 389 boats crossed the line. Thousands of sailors, families and supporters camp at the club for the weekend.

Other fixtures include Keelboat Week, hosted at Deneysville Aquatic Club, and regular club racing almost every weekend. Not a sailor? Many clubs welcome newcomers and learners; ask about their open days and courses.

Angling

Fishing the Vaal Dam

Drive along almost any stretch of shoreline and you will find rods propped on the rocks. The dam is a favourite of carp and barbel anglers, and its main angling species include barbel (sharptooth catfish), common, mirror and grass carp, smallmouth and largemouth yellowfish and mudfish.

Night sessions for barbel, early mornings for carp, a boat out to the drop-offs: there are as many ways to fish here as there are anglers. Largemouth yellowfish are a threatened and much-prized species, so please practise catch-and-release with them.

Before you cast: make sure you have the provincial angling licence for the shore you fish from (Gauteng or Free State), and respect private property and club waters.

Speed & spray

Waterskiing, wakeboarding & jet skiing

Get out early, when the water is glassy and the bays are quiet, and the Vaal Dam is a skier’s dream. Waterskiing, wakeboarding, tubing, jet skiing and paddling all have their fans, and the dam has hosted big events such as long-distance jet ski races.

Launch from a public or resort slipway, keep to the rules of the water, give sailors and anglers plenty of room, and always wear a life jacket. See our water safety tips.

Birding

Birding around the dam

Listen for the ringing call of the African fish eagle: a few dozen live around the dam. Egyptian geese and blacksmith lapwings patrol the shore, Caspian terns gather in large numbers, and greater flamingos wade the shallows, sometimes joined by lesser flamingos. Ospreys turn up now and then.

The dam is part of the wider Vaal birding route, which takes in grassland, wetlands, pans and riverine bush from Devon to the Vredefort Dome. More than 360 bird species have been recorded across the route. Nearby highlights include Marievale Bird Sanctuary and Suikerbosrand Nature Reserve.

On foot

Hiking & nature

Lace up for a shoreline walk at sunrise, when mist lifts off the water and the birds are busiest. For proper trails, Suikerbosrand Nature Reserve near Heidelberg offers rolling grassland, rocky ridges and day hikes with zebra, eland and other game, a short drive north of the dam.

Further down the river, the Vredefort Dome has hiking routes through the hills of a UNESCO World Heritage Site (more below).

Slow down

More Vaal experiences

Sunsets

Big skies and open water make for spectacular sunsets. Find a west-facing shore, light the braai and watch the colours roll in.

Photography

Golden hour, storm clouds, sailing fleets and fish eagles: bring a long lens and a wide one.

Cycling

Quiet farm roads and open Highveld scenery around the dam suit road and gravel riders.

parkrun

Weekly free timed 5 km runs take place in the wider Vaal area, including Riviera on Vaal. Check parkrun’s site for the latest venues.

Riverside living

Some of the best Vaal days involve nothing more than a deck chair, a cold drink and the sound of water.

History hunting

Look for flying-boat foundations and old farm walls when the water is low, and visit the sandstone monuments of Frankfort.

Upstream

Above the dam: the Wilge and upper Vaal

Follow the water upstream and the landscape turns to farmland, poplars and slow brown rivers. The Wilge River, which carries water from the eastern Free State (and, through the Lesotho Highlands scheme, from Lesotho) into the dam, flows past the historic town of Frankfort, with its riverside resort, picnic spots and sandstone national monuments. Frankfort has also hosted a popular Wilge River raft race and marathon.

On the main Vaal River above the dam, the town of Villiers sits beside the N3, a handy stop between Johannesburg and Durban and a gateway to the quieter upper reaches of the river.

Downstream

Below the dam: the Barrage, Parys and the Vredefort Dome

Below the wall the Vaal River winds west past the Vaal Triangle towns and into some of the most beautiful river country in the interior.

Vereeniging & the Barrage

The river towns of Vereeniging and Vanderbijlpark are known for riverside resorts, boating and restaurants with a view. The Vaal River Barrage, built by Rand Water in the early 1920s, holds back a long, navigable stretch of the river.

Parys

A lively Free State river town where the Vaal breaks into islands, rocks and rapids. A favourite for river rafting, tubing, galleries, antique shops and long lunches.

Vredefort Dome

The remains of an ancient meteorite impact structure, among the largest and oldest known on Earth, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2005. Hike the hills, paddle the river and stay in the bush.

The water is waiting. Come and find your Vaal.

Pack the cooler box, load the rods or the wakeboard, and point the car south. Whether it is a sunrise at Vaal Marina, a sail out of Deneysville, a quiet weekend at Oranjeville or a stroll past Frankfort's sandstone monuments, the Vaal Dam is closer than you think.
