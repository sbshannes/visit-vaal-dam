Deneysville on the Vaal Dam

Sails, history and easy access to the open water: welcome to the largest town on the Vaal Dam.

Photo: Ossewa, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons. Credits

Deneysville, on the Free State shore close to the dam wall, was laid out in 1936 as the dam was filling and named after Deneys Reitz, the Boer War soldier, author of Commando and later cabinet minister. Today it is the largest town on the dam, and with its yacht clubs, marinas and slipways it is easy to see why so many think of it as the dam’s water-sport capital.

Sailing runs deep here. Lake Deneys Yacht Club hosts the record-breaking Round the Island Race every February, and Keelboat Week is sailed from Deneysville Aquatic Club. The National Sea Rescue Institute’s Station 22 is based in the town, a reminder of just how big this inland sea is.

Deneysville is also where the flying boats came ashore. From 1948 to 1950, BOAC Solents from Southampton landed on the dam here, and the foundations of the old terminal still appear when the water is very low. Across the bay lies UJ Island.

History runs deeper still. The ruins of stone dwellings built by Sotho-Tswana communities who lived along the river in the nineteenth century can be seen in the area, and the stone cottage on the waterfront that was once a Reitz family hunting lodge is now St Peter’s Church, a much-loved wedding venue.

Why visit

  • Yacht clubs, regattas and the Round the Island Race
  • Flying-boat history and stone-walled heritage sites
  • Fishing, boating and camping right on the water
  • About 100 kilometres from Johannesburg

Deneysville’s historic motorcycle collection has been a draw for bikers over the years. Please check that it is open before you plan a visit around it.
