Frankfort

Sandstone, history and a slow river on the upper Vaal system.

Photo: Eugeneferreira, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons. Credits

Frankfort is a farming town on the banks of the Wilge River in the north-eastern Free State. The Wilge is the big tributary that joins the Vaal at the head of the dam, so a visit here takes you upstream into the country that feeds the lake. The town was laid out in 1869 on the farm Roodepoort and is the seat of the Mafube municipality, which includes Villiers, Cornelia and Tweeling.

Frankfort is a pleasure to wander. In Van Reenen Street, three historic buildings stand side by side: the Edwardian Magistrate’s Office, the sandstone Police Station and the 1904 Post Office with its Cape Dutch gable. All three were declared national monuments. Nearby, the Dutch Reformed Church has its own story: President Johannes Brand of the Orange Free State laid its cornerstone in 1883, it was burnt during the Anglo-Boer War, and it was rebuilt and inaugurated in 1918. Its old bell survived the fire.

Down at the river there is a resort and caravan park with picnic spots on the banks, and the Wilge is good for angling, swimming and paddling. In Namahadi township, the Art and Crafts Centre has long showcased local crafts and beadwork. Frankfort sits about 30 kilometres off the N3, an easy detour for travellers who prefer the road less travelled.

Why visit

  • Sandstone national monuments in Van Reenen Street
  • The Wilge River, upstream of the Vaal Dam
  • Local crafts at Namahadi
  • About two hours from Johannesburg, close to the N3
